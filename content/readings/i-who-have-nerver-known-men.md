This book made me think of memories, and how they fade away if you do not constantly narrate or recall them. This then connects to a sense of identity, and it is only built by constantly telling about (or reflecting on) your life. Without telling it, either to another person, or internalizing it in text, you're only left fragments or disparate sensory reminders, and no story.

In the novel, the narrator lives in an eternal, flat present once all her companions die. When there is no one left who remembers what happened before, then the narrator's memories of their tales would also evaporate. Writing becomes a way for her to prove that she occupies time and space. If nothing around her changes, and no one is there to remember her actions, writing creates the only record that one moment was different from another. So our sense of self is then defined by how others perceive/react or just witness us? When the narrator is alone on the plains after the others die, she loses the social structure. There was nobody there to correct her grammar, answer her questions, or just listen to her. Anyway, it was a depressing read.
